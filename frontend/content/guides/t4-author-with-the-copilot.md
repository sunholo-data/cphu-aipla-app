::: callout-note
## Where this fits

The AI **co-pilot** lives on the activity pages — the three steps of a new
activity and the editor (guide *T2*). Instead of filling in every field by
hand, you describe what you want to teach and the co-pilot proposes a goal and
tools. You review each
proposal and decide what to keep. Nothing changes until you approve it.
:::

## What the co-pilot does

The co-pilot on the activity pages (labelled **Build helper**; **Hjælp til at
bygge** when your screens are in Danish) reads a plain-language description of your lesson and drafts:

- a **goal** — what the students should learn,
- **tools** for the workspace — a checklist, a note, a table, a chart, a
  calculator, and so on — or a **simulation** from the library,
- a **concept map** for the activity,
- suggested **curriculum materials** to add (guide *T3*), and how to file the
  activity (subject, level, tags),
- what **students see first**: an introduction and suggestions students can
  tap (guide *T5*).

It replies in the language you write in.

Each suggestion arrives as a **proposal** you can apply, edit, or dismiss. You
stay in control: the co-pilot never changes the activity on its own.

## Step 1 — Open the co-pilot

Start a new activity or open one in the editor (guide *T2*). Click the
**✨ Build helper** button at the bottom-right of the page — the panel opens
there, ready for a description. The first time you visit, a short tip beside
the button says what it can do. You can also open it from **Help** in the
header, which lists the page's own helper below **Guides** and **Ask AIPLA
Help**.

![The co-pilot panel sits at the bottom-right of the activity page.](/guides/assets/t4-01-copilot-panel.png)

## Step 2 — Describe what you want to teach

Type a short description — for example, *"energy conservation for a 2.g class,
with a checklist and a worked example"* — and select **Send**. The co-pilot
thinks for a moment, then returns one or more proposals.

## Step 3 — Review each proposal

Each proposal is a card: a suggested goal (*Suggestion: What should students
learn?*), a checklist, a note, a table, and so on. For each one you can:

- **Apply** — accept the suggestion into your draft,
- **Edit** — adjust it first, then select **Use this**, or
- **Dismiss** — reject it.

![Each suggestion is a proposal card you can apply, edit, or dismiss.](/guides/assets/t4-02-proposal.png)

## Step 4 — Apply and refine

When you apply a proposal, it drops into the matching field of the page — and
stays fully editable. Applied suggestions are marked so you can see what the
co-pilot contributed. Keep refining by hand, or ask for more.

![Applied suggestions land on the page and remain editable.](/guides/assets/t4-03-applied.png)

When the activity looks right, create it (**Create and give to** *your class*)
or, in the editor, **Save** it (guide *T2*). Nothing the co-pilot proposed is
live until then.

::: callout-tip
The co-pilot proposes; you dispose. Every change waits for your **Apply**,
and even applied changes remain editable before you save — so it is
safe to explore its suggestions freely.
:::
