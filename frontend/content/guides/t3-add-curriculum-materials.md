::: callout-note
## Where this fits

Curriculum materials are cited **inside an activity** (guide *T2*). Open an
activity, go to its **Materials** section, and attach the documents you want
the tutor to draw on. To upload and file documents without opening an
activity, use **Materials** in the teacher navigation — the same library,
without the activity-specific controls.

The labels below are the English ones. The teacher screens follow the
**DA | EN** switch; in Danish the section is called **Materialer**.
:::

## What materials do

When you attach a document to an activity, the tutor can **cite it** — ground
its answers in your source rather than in general knowledge. You can attach:

- documents from the **shared corpus** — material already available to you, and
- your **own uploads** — a PDF, slides, a worksheet, notes, or an image.

Each attached document is either **visible** to students or **hidden** (used
only to ground the tutor), and either **Reference** (the tutor looks it up when
relevant) or **In context** (the tutor is given the full text on every turn).
You choose per document.

## Step 1 — Open the Materials section

Open an activity for editing (or create one — guide *T2*). In the builder's
section navigation, select **Materials**. The section lists the shared corpus
you can browse, with an **Upload** button and controls to filter and organise.

![The Materials section: browse the shared corpus, upload your own, filter by subject, folder, level or tag.](/guides/assets/t3-01-materials.png)

## Step 2 — Attach a document

You have two ways to attach material:

- **Cite an existing document.** Use the **Search materials** box or the
  **Subject**, **Folders**, **Level** and **Tags** filters to find what you
  want, then select **Cite** on its row. It moves into the activity's cited
  list.
- **Upload your own.** Select **Upload** and choose a file (PDF, Word, slides,
  spreadsheet, plain text, or an image). A document is cited straight away, and
  AIPLA shows you **what it extracted**, so you can confirm it was read
  correctly before relying on it. An image is not text-extracted — the tutor
  sees the picture itself; save the activity once before uploading one.

![After an upload, AIPLA shows the extracted text so you can verify the parse.](/guides/assets/t3-02-extracted.png)

Select a document's title at any time to see what was extracted from it again.

## Step 3 — Organise: folders, tags and subject

Keep a growing corpus navigable:

- **Folders** — group related documents; create one with **New** in the
  **Folders** row. Deleting a folder unfiles its documents rather than deleting
  them.
- **Tags** and **Subject** — select **Add tags** (or **Edit**) on a document to
  set its subject, folder and tags, so the filters can find it later. On the
  **Materials** page the same button reads **Organise**.

An upload is filed into whatever subject and folder you are filtered to at the
time. Organisation on the shared corpus is shared, so a document you tag well
is easier for you and your colleagues to reuse across activities. Your own
uploads stay in your own library; only researchers can add an upload to the
shared library. The bin icon on a row deletes a document from the library.

## Step 4 — Control what students see, and what the tutor always has

Each cited document has two toggles:

- **Visible / Hidden** — hidden documents still ground the tutor's answers;
  visible documents also appear to students in the activity. Newly cited
  documents start **Hidden** — make one visible only when you want students to
  read the source directly.
- **Reference / In context** — **Reference** (the default) lets the tutor look
  the document up when it is relevant, which suits a textbook. **In context**
  gives the tutor the full text on every turn: use it for the task your
  students are working on, so the tutor never has to search for it or ask them
  to paste it in.

::: callout-tip
Grounding works whether a document is visible or hidden. Use *hidden* for
source material that should shape the tutor's answers without being handed to
students verbatim.
:::
