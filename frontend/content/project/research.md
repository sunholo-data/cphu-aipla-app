# Research

AIPLA investigates how generative AI can support learning and assessment in upper-secondary physics without replacing the reasoning, experimentation, and communication students need to practise themselves.

## Research questions

The project studies how generative AI can support students in four connected areas.

### Solving physics problems

Can an AI tutor offer timely questions, hints, and representations without simply producing the solution? The project examines how different kinds of scaffolding affect students' reasoning and independence.

### Carrying out experiments

Physics knowledge is practical as well as conceptual. AIPLA explores whether AI can support experimental design, troubleshooting, measurement, and data interpretation while keeping students engaged with the physical situation.

### Exploring concepts

AI can act as a conversational counterpart when students make predictions, compare representations, and explain phenomena. The research asks which conversational patterns help students develop and revise their understanding.

### Preparing presentations

Oral and written communication are important parts of Danish physics education and assessment. AIPLA investigates appropriate roles for AI when students plan, explain, and critique physics presentations.

## Principles guiding the work

### Learning before automation

The goal is not to maximise AI use. Activities are designed around a learning purpose, and AI is used only where it can contribute productively.

### Teacher control

Teachers choose the activity, source material, learning goals, and tutor configuration. Student sessions take place within that prepared context rather than in an unrestricted chatbot.

### Physics-specific design

The project treats equations, diagrams, graphs, experiments, simulations, and verbal explanations as connected representations of physics. Tutors and activities are designed around those relationships.

### Responsible research practice

Data protection, transparency, accessibility, and clear boundaries for AI-generated content are part of the design process. The platform uses group-based student access and is being developed with institutional review and classroom research requirements in mind.

## How the research develops

The project moves through repeated cycles with teachers and students. Early prototypes test a broad range of activity types. Findings from classroom use then determine which approaches deserve deeper development and formal evaluation.

Outputs are expected to include peer-reviewed research, teacher resources, seminars, open documentation, and reusable software where release is compatible with institutional and data-protection requirements.
