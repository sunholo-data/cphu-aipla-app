# About AIPLA

AIPLA — Artificial Intelligence in Physics Learning and Assessment — is a three-year research project investigating how generative AI can be integrated meaningfully into upper-secondary physics education in Denmark.

The project is hosted by the Department of Science Education at the University of Copenhagen and runs from 2026 to 2028 with support from the Novo Nordisk Foundation. It is led by Associate Professor Jesper Bruun and developed in close collaboration with Danish physics teachers and Danske Science Gymnasier.

## Why the project exists

Generative AI can already solve many conventional physics exercises. That creates both opportunities and difficult questions for teaching: when does an AI system help a student reason, experiment, and communicate more effectively, and when does it simply replace the work through which learning happens?

AIPLA focuses on activities where AI cannot — and should not — do everything. The project explores how AI can support students while preserving the conceptual understanding, mathematical reasoning, experimental practice, and use of multiple representations that make physics a distinctive subject.

## What AIPLA is building

The project combines research with practical development. Teachers and researchers work together to:

- design AI-supported physics activities and assessments;
- develop configurable tutors and interactive simulations;
- test the activities in authentic classroom settings;
- study how teachers and students experience and use them; and
- create practical resources that can be shared with the wider physics-teaching community.

The platform is a research environment rather than a general-purpose chatbot. Teachers choose the activity, learning goals, curriculum material, and form of support before students enter a session.

## Project approach

AIPLA follows an iterative **Analyse, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate** cycle:

1. **Analyse** curricula, current AI capabilities, and teachers' needs.
2. **Design** activities, tutor behaviour, research instruments, and safeguards.
3. **Develop** the platform, simulations, and teaching resources.
4. **Implement** them with teachers and students in classroom settings.
5. **Evaluate** interactions, outcomes, and participant perspectives, then use the findings to improve the next cycle.
