# Project progress

AIPLA began its current development and classroom-preparation phase in May 2026. This page provides a public, high-level view of the work. Detailed delivery plans, research notes, and infrastructure documentation remain in the project's internal working materials.

## 2026: platform and pilot preparation

The first phase established the technical platform and produced working examples for discussion with physics teachers. Early activities included projectile motion, a virtual LED experiment, and kinematics simulations.

The platform now supports:

- anonymous group-based student access;
- teacher-created classes and activities;
- guided AI tutors grounded in prepared teaching material;
- interactive simulations alongside the conversation;
- document and image use within activities; and
- research and teacher views for reviewing classroom sessions.

## Collaboration with teachers

Teacher feedback has shaped the platform from the beginning. Early reviews focused on practical classroom use, the balance between hints and answers, support for multiple representations, and the need for teachers to remain in control of activity design.

The next phase moves from internal development into teacher workshops and classroom pilots. Findings from those pilots will determine which activity types are developed further.

## Research and evaluation

Alongside the classroom work, the project is developing ways to evaluate AI systems on physics-specific tasks. The purpose is to understand which models and configurations are sufficiently capable for particular forms of support, not to produce a general model leaderboard.

Evaluation material will be published when its methods, datasets, and interpretation are ready for research review.

## What comes next

Priorities for the next stage include:

- classroom testing with teachers and students;
- refinement of teacher authoring and review tools;
- stronger privacy, consent, and institutional hosting arrangements;
- analysis of how students use tutors and simulations together; and
- development of reusable teacher resources based on the findings.

For announcements and the formal project description, follow the [Department of Science Education](https://www.ind.ku.dk/).
