> **Aside, not triage.** Continues the 25 August thread — *"how to engineer the
> tool. paper publications. open source."*
>
> - **Material already exists** and was written for outside readers: the
>   [capability-floor report](design/aipla/v2.0.0-handover/capability-floor-for-ku-ai-office.qmd)
>   (already pitched at an institutional rather than physics audience), the
>   [UCPH hosting requirements](design/aipla/v2.0.0-handover/ucph-it-hosting-requirements.qmd),
>   ADRs 001–015, the four-protocol stack, and the
>   [retrospective](design/aipla/retrospective-2026-05-to-09.md). The pilot gives
>   real numbers (12 teachers, 22 groups, 334 turns, 30-of-30 clean joins).
> - **This makes A4 urgent rather than eventual.** `ku-ai-office-alignment.md`
>   lists A4 as *"settle the licensing/ownership position of the upstream
>   template, so Option 2 is answerable if asked"* — a published overview invites
>   exactly that question from strangers, not just from KU, and the answer wants
>   to exist before the paper does.
> - **Consent to use the tool is not consent to be published.** If the paper
>   reports pilot data, that is a second and different permission from anything
>   the students agreed to — and it is the third item in these notes to land on
>   the missing DPIA.
> - Authorship/anonymisation: the project convention is initials (M, JB, AR, DS,
>   ZL, K), which a paper obviously reverses. Worth settling who is named.
>
> **On the differentiation question — axes, not answers.** The comparison needs
> real desk research on what other tools actually do today; nothing in this repo
> knows that, and it should not be guessed at. What the repo *can* supply is the
> list of design decisions AIPLA made that are unusual enough to be worth testing
> a claim against:
>
> - **The group, not the student, is the unit** (ADR-001). Anonymous group IDs,
>   no student PII, shared table and session. Most tutoring tools are 1:1 and
>   identity-bound; this was taken for GDPR reasons and turned into a pedagogical
>   position.
> - **The tutor can see what the student did in the simulation** — element state
>   is pushed into the tutor's context, so it reacts to a sim run or a table of
>   readings, not only to typed text.
> - **Visible "shared with the AI" trust cards** — the student is told what the
>   model can see, structurally rather than as a policy claim.
> - **Teachers author the activity**, with an AI co-pilot that proposes and never
>   applies. Not prompt templates.
> - **A self-hosting path with an evidence base** (ADR-003 tiers + the
>   capability-floor eval), rather than an assumption that the frontier API is
>   the only option.
> - **Logs as a research instrument** — rubric-scored, `revision`-stamped for A/B.
>
> Related framing already written:
> [ku-ai-office-alignment.md](design/aipla/v2.0.0-handover/ku-ai-office-alignment.md)
> §"The evidence base, and its limits" and the Option-2 "template" argument, which
> is a differentiation case in all but name.
