# The extension — plan for 2026-09-01 → 2027-04-30

**Status**: **Draft for M, 2026-09-01.** Internal planning, not a programme deliverable.
**Input**: [retrospective-2026-05-to-09.md](../retrospective-2026-05-to-09.md) · [handover-package.md](../v2.0.0-handover/handover-package.md) · [ku-ai-office-alignment.md](../v2.0.0-handover/ku-ai-office-alignment.md)
**Horizon**: the committed extension boundary — at least April 2027 at 2.5 days/week.

## The reframe this plan is built on

The old plan ended on 2026-09-15 and every document written before August
inherits that shape. Under the extension:

- **09-15 is a checkpoint inside the plan, not its end.** It still has a
  contracted bar and that bar should be met — but meeting it is two weeks of
  this plan, not its purpose.
- **AD is a colleague, not a successor.** `outputs/handover/README.md` says AD
  *"starts ~1 Oct; no in-person overlap with M, so these documents carry the
  handover."* **That was written against the old boundary and is now false in
  its load-bearing half**: AD starts ~1 Oct and M is present to at least April,
  so there are **~6 months of overlap**. In-person may still be nil, but "the
  documents are all AD gets" is not the constraint any more. Onboarding AD by
  *pairing* is now cheaper and better than onboarding by document — and the
  runbooks change from a farewell package into a working aid AD can help write.
- **The strategic call is already made and should be honoured.**
  `ku-ai-office-alignment.md`: *"spend the extension on the discipline layer, not
  the shell. Activity authoring, the sims/artefact surface, curriculum RAG on
  cleared material, rubric-scored logs as assessment evidence. A central KU
  platform absorbs a chat shell and cannot absorb any of those."*

**Compare with what has just happened: 1,143 commits in 15 weeks at what was

capacity of the contract window it follows. Any plan that reads like the last

**Implication: the extension can afford roughly two substantial workstreams plus
maintenance — not five.**

### The reordering this forces

**Two legal gates, both owned by JB, both targeted Nov–Dec, and both blocking
work involving students.** The first draft sequenced the feedback residue and the
question-set element against a November that turns out to mean "teachers start
teaching C-level", not "students use the system".

So the honest position: **most of the 1.1.90–1.1.98 batch serves a classroom that
does not properly open until 2027.** Building it first would be building ahead of
a gate held by someone else.

**The reorder principle: spend the wait on what pays off *without* students.**
Three things qualify, and between them they fill the gap:

1. **Researcher-facing instruments** (1.1.91 tutors, 1.1.92 benchmark) — they need
   researchers and existing data, not new classrooms, and they are the project's
   research output.
2. **Teacher-facing work** (1.1.96 UI friction, 1.1.95 safe-to-publish, activity
   examples) — teachers are already using the system.
3. **The documentation deliverables** (system overview, differentiation, the
   paper, the handover runbooks) — no dependency on anyone.

**And compliance leads**, because it is on the critical path for everything else
and M is not the owner — which means the only thing M can do is make sure it does
not slip silently.

**A′ — the runbook decision, and it is a real one.** The five stubs were sized as
a farewell package. With AD arriving into a 6-month overlap, writing all five
*before* AD arrives is probably waste: the two whose reader is AD (model routing,
incident playbook) are better written **with** AD in October, against real
questions, than guessed at in September.

**Recommendation:** write the **three AR-facing / already-covered ones** now
(onboard a teacher, add a bot configuration, run the capability-floor eval —
each mostly assembly from existing material), and **defer AD's two into
Workstream B as a pairing artefact.** Saves ~1 day in the tightest fortnight and
produces better runbooks. Note the deviation from the contracted list explicitly
at the handover session rather than quietly.

### What moved, and why

- **1.1.96 (UI friction + client error reporting) pulled forward into October.**
  It needs only teachers, and its M-1 — *we have no client error visibility at
  all* — is a half-day that may explain part of "the UI is difficult" outright.
- **[1.1.99](../v1.1.0-feedback/live-class-work-wall.md) (visual live class view)
  joins F, and is the strongest candidate in the whole batch.** It answers the
  teacher ask that has now been made three times, it needs **only teachers** so
  no legal gate touches it, and its enabler shipped on 2026-08-31 — until 1.1.88
  moved the data table off `sessionStorage`, a class-wide view of student work
  had nowhere to read from. The work is already in Firestore, per group, live;
  the class view renders a coloured dot. ~4–6d.
- **1.1.91 + 1.1.92 are now the named discipline-layer content.** They were
  "choose in November" before; the transcript makes them the obvious choice —
  research-based tutors and a session benchmark were called *"a key project
  outcome"*, and they need no classroom.
- **1.1.78 question-set pushed back.** It was un-deferred on the strength of a
  November student date that does not exist. It is the exit-ticket surface and it
  should land *before* the 2027 trials, not before a gate.
- **1.1.90 / 1.1.97 / 1.1.98 stay unscheduled.** 1.1.98 moves if JB's conceptual
  framework arrives early — it is the thing that gives that framework somewhere
  to live.
- **G is new and deliberately small.** Two days of keeping two legal items
  visible, because a gate owned by someone else and targeted "Nov–Dec" is exactly
  the kind of thing that is still open in February if nobody is watching.

## What this plan deliberately does not do

- **Match the last window's output.** Half the weekly rate; a plan that reads
  like May–August is a plan that fails in November.
- **Build breadth.** The teachers asked for ergonomics and depth. New surfaces
  need to beat that bar to earn a slot.
- **Treat 09-15 as a wall.** Two weeks of work, then the plan continues.
- **Commit to the KU platform.** E is opportunistic alignment around the
  programme, not a deliverable to anyone at KU.
